Experience Audit
The BLVD at Harding
Construction is ahead of belief. The building is rising on schedule. The story of what it will feel like to walk in still hasn't been told, and right now, other people are telling it instead.
The Belief Layer
Ground Has Broken. Belief Hasn't Caught Up.

A shovel in the dirt proves a project is real to the people standing there. It does not prove the project is real to the tens of thousands of North Baton Rouge residents who read about an $18 million funding veto weeks later and heard nothing further from the development itself. The BLVD at Harding has physical momentum. What it does not yet have is a communication system built to carry that momentum through disruption, funding setbacks included.

Executive Summary
The Silence After the Veto Is the Finding

The BLVD at Harding earned genuine public excitement before a single wall went up. Residents wrote to their newspaper calling the grocery store's arrival hard earned and long overdue. Then the Governor vetoed the state capital outlay funding and the special taxing district built to help finance the project, and the development's own communication channels went quiet at the exact moment people needed to hear from them most. A political commentary outlet filled that silence with a competing frame, describing the funding as an unaccountable subsidy rather than a community's answer to a fifty year wait. The people who need to believe in this project already want to. The gap is that nobody is hearing from The BLVD at Harding itself about what the veto changes, what survives it, and when the doors open.

Current Communication Assessment
A Leasing Brochure Standing In for a Community's Story

The project's website speaks in the register of commercial real estate: essential services, ground floor retail, convenient access. That language is accurate, and it is also the wrong register for a project whose real story is a decades long absence of a full service grocer north of Airline Highway. The site's social links point to its website builder's own placeholder accounts rather than the project's real channels, and the page reserved for news and press sits empty. At the moment residents are actively searching for word on what the funding veto means for the project, the pages built to answer them lead nowhere.

Belief Gap Analysis
Where Belief Holds. Where It Breaks.
Trust DEVELOPING

The materials read as sincere. But the site has not addressed the one story now defining the project's public perception, and unaddressed news reads as something to hide.

Excitement DEVELOPING

Real excitement exists and is documented in reader letters and local coverage. The project's own communication hasn't matched that register; it lists amenities rather than naming the wait that made them matter.

Confidence DEVELOPING

Unanimous zoning approval and a groundbreaking attended by the Governor are strong signals of confidence. A blank press page and dead social links undercut them at the worst possible moment to look uncertain.

Anticipation MISSING

No opening date, construction timeline, or milestone tracker appears anywhere on the project's own site. The fall 2027 target exists only in state legislative reporting, never from the development itself.

Emotional Investment DEVELOPING

Proximity to Southern University and the end of a long standing food desert are the most powerful parts of this story. On the project's site, they appear as a feature in a list, not as the reason the project exists.

Strengths and Opportunities
The Hard Part Is Already Done

Physical progress here is not a claim, it is a public record: unanimous planning and zoning approval, a groundbreaking attended by the Governor, a Congressman, and the state senator who carried the funding bill, and an anchor grocer choosing Harding Boulevard for its very first location. A community voting feature already invites resident input on future tenants, proof the development group understands that engagement matters. The opportunity here is narrower than most audits find. This project does not need to build belief from nothing. It needs to stop leaving belief to be built in channels it doesn't control.

Priority Recommendations
Speak First, Before the Story Speaks for Itself

Publish a direct, plain language statement on what the funding veto changes and what it does not, before another outlet frames it first. Reconnect the site's social links and news page to real, active channels. Replace the amenities list with the wait Scotlandville and Southern University residents have carried, and what ends when the doors open. Put a visible construction timeline on the site itself, so the fall 2027 target comes from the people building it, not from a bill tracker.

Strategic Next Steps
The Next Sixty Days

In the next two weeks: publish the funding update and repair the site's dead links. Within thirty days: replace the homepage's feature list with a page built around the walk from Southern University's campus to the store. Within sixty days: put a construction milestone tracker live, so every future piece of news, welcome or unwelcome, lands first on a channel the project already owns instead of one it has to react to.

"A grocery store this close to Southern University was never just about groceries. It was about whether North Baton Rouge would have to wait one more year. The building will answer that question eventually. The website should be answering it now."
